# Geometric framing

This model treats ecosystem stability as a property of 
energy basins in phase space rather than a property of 
individual species.

Key structural features:

**Basin depth** corresponds to the energetic distance 
between current system state and nearest bifurcation 
threshold. Deep basins require large perturbations to 
reorganize. Shallow basins reorganize under small forcing.

**System frequency** is determined by dominant organism 
time constants. Long-lived species produce low-frequency 
dynamics. Short-lived species produce high-frequency dynamics.
Warming compresses time constants, raising system frequency.

**Trophic mismatch** occurs because Q10 values differ 
across trophic levels. Base-of-pyramid processes accelerate 
faster than apex processes. Energy interception at lower 
levels increases before apex demand can adjust.

**Ecological memory** is the temporal integration window 
of the system. Compression of the window lets
high-frequency environmental variation propagate further
into the food web unchecked.

That claim is now tested rather than asserted. Adults
integrate every recruitment year between maturity and
death, making the population a low-pass filter whose
cutoff warming shortens. ENSO-band variance reaching
adults rises about 73% between baseline and +6 °C — the
claim holds. But attenuation is roughly 1150-fold at
baseline and 670-fold at +6 °C, so the honest statement
is that the slow integrator does not stop being slow, it
stops being quite as slow. See `structure.md`.

Lifespan was the original operational definition. It is
no longer — it is one component of a multi-dimensional
life-history position, and reading sensitivity to change
off that position alone can mislead. Memory is now
measured from the projection matrix as the damping
timescale, with generation time as the integration
window and the transient period as the ringing
frequency. At baseline these are 498, 206 and 209 years
against a lifespan of 300. See `structure.md`.

**Spatial autocorrelation** determines effective landscape 
continuity for large-bodied species. Below connectivity 
thresholds, fragmentation costs compound nonlinearly.
This is now a lattice rather than a scalar, and the
threshold is emergent rather than imposed. Two
consequences that a scalar could not carry: a habitat
band thin in one dimension fragments at a higher bond
probability than a compact one of the same area, and
sectors fragmenting at different rates turn a single
threshold into a staircase — habitat retreats to
refugia rather than failing at once.

**Basins are plural.** Depth is a potential barrier in a
double well, and neighbouring basins couple
destabilisingly: a basin that reorganises raises the
effective forcing on its neighbours. Depth goes negative
when the attractor ceases to exist, which is a different
statement from an energy deficit. Cascading
destabilisation is not expressible in a single basin at
any parameter value.

These are not independent variables. They interact.
Temperature forcing affects basin depth, system frequency, 
trophic mismatch, and ecological memory simultaneously.

The model is designed to make these interactions visible,
not to predict specific outcomes.
